

Seven years. Seven long years of waiting! And to think that the day has finally come. Never would I have imagined it would arrive so soon… or perhaps I had wished for it so fervently that time itself had at last yielded to my prayers.

Who could have believed it? Me! Saraphina Hawthorn, daughter of a baron, soon to be Queen of Genland! Queen! Even now, the word seems almost too extraordinary to be true. And yet, tomorrow, I shall wear that crown. Tomorrow, I shall marry the man of my life. My Tristan. The man who has loved me so dearly, who has made me so many promises, and for whom I would willingly have crossed a thousand hells, had he but asked me to do so.

Admittedly, our path has not always been strewn with roses. We have endured hardships, passed through rather dark hours, and committed a few deeds that even the most virtuous souls would undoubtedly condemn. But what do they know of love? What could they possibly understand of the sacrifices it sometimes demands? Tristan promised me the throne. He promised me! And never, never once, had I doubted his word.

After all, how could one doubt the man one loves?

A promise made by a lover can accomplish the strangest of miracles: it can soothe one's anxieties, silence one's conscience, and even make one's transgressions seem a little more bearable. At least, that is what I believed.

"Coachman! Take me directly to my father's cathedral. I must speak with His Highness, Tristan."

"At once, Madam!"

Ah, Tristan! I had so hoped to see him before the ceremony. Seven years spent waiting for this very moment, and yet a few remaining hours seemed an eternity. Was it truly so unreasonable to wish for a glimpse of my future husband before becoming his queen?

Oh, I had so much to tell him! And even more to ask… although, now that I think of it, a single glance from him would probably make me forget half of my carefully prepared speeches.

But what did it matter? Tomorrow, we will finally be united before the entire kingdom. No one would be able to question our union any longer. No one would ever separate us again.

Well… no one who had the power to do so.

My mother had sent me a letter. How peculiar! Perhaps she wished to mend our relationship at last. After all these years, she had suddenly remembered that she had a daughter. How touching! Could it be that my future crown had finally reminded her of my existence?

I was not yet certain whether I was prepared to forgive her. Forgiveness is a grace not easily granted, particularly when one has been neglected for so long. Nevertheless, I could afford to be merciful. After all, I would soon be queen. It would hardly become me to appear resentful… though I had never claimed to be a saint.

Everything would depend on her attitude towards me. We shall see.

Oh! How beautiful the sky was that evening!

A gentle darkness had settled over the streets, while the heavens seemed adorned in their finest ornaments, as though they, too, wished to celebrate the happiness that awaited me. The stars glittered above. Everything appeared so peaceful, so perfectly in its place.

For the first time in a very long while, I felt as though the world belonged to me.

And yet… Wait. What is that? The moon… It is red.

A deep, almost bloodied red, as though the heavens themselves had been wounded.

What a curious sight! I had never witnessed such a color. Could it be an omen? Some foolish superstition, perhaps? Oh, what nonsense! I really must stop frightening myself over the smallest things. Tomorrow will be the happiest day of my life. Nothing, absolutely nothing, could possibly spoil it.

Everything would be fine. I was certain of it. Tom—

BOOOOM!

What was happening? Where had that dreadful noise come from? And why was there such an uproar outside?

"Coachman? What is happening?"

No answer. Silence. A cold, heavy silence, almost unreal. What? Not a sound? Not a single voice? Where had everyone gone? The coachman? The guards? Someone, for heaven's sake.

What could it be? Bandits, perhaps? But what could they possibly be after at such an hour? And, more importantly, did they have the slightest idea where they were?

How utterly insolent! I took a deep breath before raising my voice.

"If you are here to rob this place, you ought to know that you are addressing the future Queen of Genland. You have chosen the wrong target, gentlemen! Leave these premises at once, and I shall consent to overlook this unfortunate incident. I am coming downstairs, and I expect to find you with your weapons lowered. Do you understand me? Your weapons lowered, I—"

No sooner had I descended the final steps than I found myself surrounded by the royal guards.

"Oh! But… what are you doing? Are you quite well? You gave me such a fright! Who sent you? Is it Tristan? Tell him that I am at my mother's residence and that I shall be perfectly on time for the ceremony. And, above all, make certain he understands that I am not running away! Good heavens, what might he think if you fail to deliver my message properly?"

I almost expected one of them to smile at my concern. But not a single man did.

One stepped forward. His expression remained utterly impassive. Not the slightest sign of respect, not even the hesitation one would naturally expect when addressing a lady of my standing.

"Miss Saraphina Hawthorn, you are hereby under arrest for the murder of the former prince. For this crime, you have been sentenced to death. Any resistance shall be considered a refusal to comply. The consequences that follow shall be entirely your responsibility."

"Pardon?"

Pardon?! Had I heard him correctly?

"I beg your… pardon?! Do you even comprehend the gravity of what you are saying? And who sent you? You are members of the royal guard, are you not? Give me your name! I shall personally see to it that this insolence is reported! The murder of the former prince? What utter absurdity! How dare they make such accusations against me? And why were they looking at me like that? As though I were… a criminal!"

"Miss, you must understand that any refusal to cooperate constitutes disobedience to His Highness's orders. Do not make your situation worse. Please follow the guards behind you."

He had not even finished speaking when several men approached from behind. Their heavy hands settled on my shoulders. I immediately pushed them away.

"No! Unhand me! Do you have the slightest idea of who you are dealing with? I am… I am the… future… Queen of this kingdom! Your future queen!"

My voice began to falter as the words escaped my lips. What humiliation! I, Saraphina Hawthorn, being treated like some common prisoner by men who, tomorrow, ought to have been bowing before me!

"Release me! Release me, you brutes!"

"Release her."

That voice. Oh, that voice! Firm, cold, and terribly familiar.

"Tristan!"

Relief swept over me so suddenly that, for a moment, I almost forgot the hands still holding me.

"You heard him! You lost! Release me!"

I pushed the guards away one last time and hurried towards him.

At last! He was here. My Tristan. He would surely put an end to this ridiculous spectacle. All he would need was a single word, and these men would finally understand their mistake.

"Tristan! What is happening? How…?"

I moved closer and leaned towards him, lowering my voice so that the others might not hear.

"How could they have discovered what we did? About us… and the prince?"

He looked at me.

I expected an explanation, perhaps a few reassuring words. That familiar expression of his, the one that had so often been enough to calm me.

But nothing.

Then, with a composure that chilled me more than the night itself, he replied:

"We?"

I blinked.

"What do you mean, we? You mean… you."

"What?"

The word escaped me. A single, ridiculous, almost childish word. I hated it the moment it left my lips. I stepped back slightly.

"Wh-what do you mean?"

No. He must have misspoken. It was impossible. Tristan could not speak to me that way. Not after seven years. Not after all his promises. Not after everything I had sacrificed for him.

I searched his eyes, hoping to find something there. An explanation. A smile. Anything that might convince me I had simply misunderstood.

But his face offered me nothing.

And suddenly, I felt something break within me. I did not understand it yet.

But, for the first time in my life, I no longer recognized the man standing before me.

"What I mean, Miss, is that you murdered the prince. I mean… the late prince."

He raised his voice, making certain that every man present could hear his words.

"No… No, I… I didn't… I…"

I turned towards Tristan, unable to comprehend what was unfolding before my eyes.

"Tristan… Please, do not do this to me. You cannot possibly do this to me! You love me, don't you? You cannot simply leave me to face them alone! Stand by me! Say something to them!"

He stared at me for a moment before letting out a bitter laugh.

"Me? How could I ever have loved such a traitorous creature? You murdered my brother! How dare you?"

My brother.

Those two words sounded so strange in my mind.

"But… you asked me to do it! You gave me the poison! You were the one who told me what to do! You… You… YOU!"

My voice suddenly rose. I slipped my hand into my sleeve and drew out the small knife I had concealed there.

"You used me!"

"Ahahahahaha!"

He burst into laughter. It was so genuine, so utterly devoid of remorse, that it took me several moments to realize he was laughing at me.

Then he fell silent. A contemptuous smile spread across his lips.

"I know all of that perfectly well. You're far too foolish to have devised such a perfect plan on your own."

His eyes lowered to the little knife in my hand.

"And what exactly do you intend to do with that little toy? What? Kill me? Come now, Saraphina… You're no match for me."

With a swift movement, he disarmed me. I stood motionless, my hands trembling.

"I thought… I thought you loved me."

My voice was little more than a whisper.

"And whom do you intend to marry tomorrow if you arrest me? Hm, Tristan? Who will take my place by your side? Surely, you cannot simply cancel our wedding with only a few hours remaining before the ceremony!"

He appeared to consider this for a moment before allowing a smile to form.

"Hm… Oh, do not trouble yourself over such matters. Your sister, Flora, will make an excellent queen. She is far better suited for the position than you, don't you think?"
