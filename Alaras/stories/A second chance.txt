

"No, Father! I will not marry Prince Alistar!"

That voice… It was my sister's. But… was I not dead?

"Twenty years for a soul. Vengeance in exchange for your soul."

So, it was true. My pact with Death. I had returned to life. But at what point in time? And, more importantly… how much time had I been granted?

I lifted my eyes and surveyed my surroundings. My father's residence, and that voice, which continued to protest…

"But, my dear child, the decree is quite clear…"

"The decree states that a Hawthorn daughter must marry the prince. It does not specify which one of us! My sister could just as easily marry him, could you not, Saraphina?"

I suppressed a sigh.

Flora. As charming as ever.

I understood then. I had returned seven years into the past, to the very day the royal decree had been proclaimed, demanding that a daughter of the Hawthorn family marry the Crown Prince.

But something was amiss. In my memories, Flora had welcomed the announcement with enthusiasm. She had always dreamed of a royal title, a crown, and everything that came with it.

Then why was she refusing? And, above all… why had I been given this opportunity?

In my previous life, I had been destined to marry Tristan, while Flora was to marry Alistar, the prince who had perished by my own hand.

But this time, I could change things.

"My dear sister… Are you willing to do so?"

Flora looked at me with an almost provocative expression.

What was I to do? If I accepted, I would end up marrying the very man I had murdered. And if I refused, I would once again be promised to Tristan.

No. Absolutely not. I would not return to that gilded prison. Not a second time.

I shall marry Prince Alistar.

"Are you certain, my daughter?" my father asked.

"Yes, Father."

He nodded slowly before turning his attention to my sister.

Later, as I made my way down the corridor, I caught sight of Tristan in the company of Flora. Their conversation came to an abrupt halt the moment they noticed me.

"Miss Saraphina," Tristan said, extending his hand. I pretended not to notice it.

"Lord Tristan. My congratulations on your engagement to my sister."

"Thank you. And my congratulations to you on your engagement to Prince Alistar."

He paused briefly before Flora dared to add, almost playfully:

"A word of advice: enjoy it while you can. He probably won't live very long."

I came to a halt

"Pardon me… What exactly do you mean by that?"

I stopped speaking and took a brief moment of silence, trying to compose my thoughts. I looked at them, searching for an answer.

"Mind your words, Lord Tristan! Sister, how could I possibly kill the man who is to become my husband?"

Tristan immediately intervened, his face displaying an almost comical expression of indignation.

"Now, now, ladies. It was merely a joke," Tristan replied.

"A joke, in exceedingly poor taste, if you ask me. Now, if you would kindly excuse me, I have other matters to attend to."

I offered them a brief curtsy before walking away, leaving them to resume their idle chatter.

Yet one thought refused to leave my mind. How could they possibly know that I was going to kill the prince? Unless…

No. Could it be that they, too, had returned? That they had also made a pact with Death?

But then, had they died before their appointed time? And for what reason? I had to remain cautious. Above all, I must give nothing away. No one must discover that I had returned.

Not until I was ready.

"Miss Saraphina!"

A distant voice startled me. I abruptly emerged from my thoughts and turned towards the footsteps approaching behind me. I found myself face to face with Prince Alistar.

I remained silent for a moment. How peculiar.

In my previous life, I had never taken the time to truly look at him. Now, I noticed his bearing, his harmonious features, and his firm, almost reassuring presence. He possessed a natural elegance, yet there was something about his gaze that seemed particularly… loyal.

And now it came to me: the last time I had seen that man was when he was suffering after I gave him that poisonous beverage. How could I marry that man—

"Miss Saraphina, are you quite well?"

"Ah… Yes! Forgive me, Your Highness. I beg your pardon. I… I greet you, Your Highness."

I hurriedly curtsied.

"Please, do rise. We are to be married soon, after all."

He gently helped me to my feet.

"Ah… yes. Of course."

"You looked rather as though you had seen a… dead man."

I raised my eyes sharply towards him.

"Pardon?"

He regarded me with an unreadable expression.

"You seem rather troubled, Miss. I do hope this marriage does not displease you."

"No! Not at all, Your Highness."

What an absurd answer.

"Excellent. I was on my way to speak with your father regarding the wedding arrangements. He is expecting me."

"I see."

"I hope to have the pleasure of seeing you again soon."

He took my hand delicately and placed a kiss upon it.

"The pleasure shall be mine, Your Highness."

Then he departed. I watched him disappear at the end of the corridor.

"Twenty years for a soul… Vengeance in exchange for your soul."

Those words continued to echo in my mind. I made myself a promise then: I would do everything in my power not to repeat the mistakes of my past.

The prince would live.

My sister Flora would pay.

And Tristan… will die by my hand.

I swore it.

And my soul would be the price.

A Few Moments Later…

(Prince Alistar's Perspective)

I continued along the corridor, my hands crossed behind my back. As I thought, Miss Hawthorn was certainly a peculiar young woman. It was interesting to see her confused. She had looked at me as though she had recognized a face she would have much preferred never to see again.

I paused for a moment, stopped in my march, and thought. Was it fear? Remorse? Or merely coincidence?

A faint smile touched my lips.

After all, certain encounters have the curious habit of making one feel as though they have happened before.

Do they not, Miss Hawthorn?
